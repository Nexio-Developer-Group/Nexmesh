Build the NIDAR baseline on the **retrieve → match → PnP → consistency gate → tracking** structure that S6 implements, with S6's permissive-licence parts (MegaLoc, XFeat, OpenCV; confirm the LighterGlue weight licence), fed by the compass and barometer hints. On the synthetic data it was the only complete pipeline: 92 % of frames fixed, 0.4 m median, 100 % precision, a 100 % mission-completion rate in SIM-2, SIM-4 and SIM-7, and a 0.9 s step on a 2-thread CPU. **Treat this as a design direction, not a verdict:** (1) S6 is our own composite, written and tuned (gate thresholds, tile size, crop) on the very frames it is scored on, with no held-out set; (2) video and map come from the same imagery, so the real appearance gap is untested; (3) nothing was measured on a Jetson, ONNX/TensorRT export of the matcher failed on CPU, and in the 5 Hz real-time replay it processed only 21 % of the frames on two threads and 13 % on one core; (4) it needs a heading and height hint, which were noise-free here. S5 (particle filter on semantic labels) was the second most accurate and the most robust to blur and haze (93.5 % fixed), but it needs an odometry source, converges only after about 10 s, takes several seconds per step on this CPU at 20,000 particles (the first step 37 s) and its labels here came from a substitute map, so it fits a GPU companion computer, not a CPU-only edge board. S1's gate and tracking ideas are already inside S6; S1 itself retrieved poorly with pretrained weights (22 % of frames, all in the first 44 s). S3 is a good reference for the plain two-stage idea but has no rotation handling and no gating; S2 (VIO plus map fixes) failed with our under-trained encoder and a VIO substitute, so it is neither supported nor refuted by this test; S4 could not be run. Whichever solution is chosen, the autopilot side needs a velocity or odometry aid: even ideal 5 m-noise fixes cannot bridge a 20 s gap with our position-only EKF configuration (SIM-3 failed for every row, including the control).
